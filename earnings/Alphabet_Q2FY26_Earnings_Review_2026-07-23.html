Headline: a clean top-line and Cloud beat, but a deceptive EPS "beat" and a much larger capex bill sent the stock down. Revenue of $119.8B beat consensus (~$116.9B) by ~2.5%, powered by Google Cloud's 82% growth — blowing past even the bull-case 70-72% flagged in our July 17 preview. But GAAP EPS of $9.11 vs. ~$2.89 consensus is not a real operating beat: it is inflated by a $99.0B non-cash mark-to-market gain (primarily Alphabet's stake in Anthropic), which by the company's own disclosure added $6.26 to diluted EPS. Strip that out and core EPS was ~$2.85 — a modest miss vs. consensus. The bigger story for the stock: management raised FY2026 capex guidance to $195-205B (from $180-190B set just in April) and quarterly free cash flow turned negative for the first time in this cycle. Shares fell ~4-5% in the reaction window and have settled ~4.3% below the July 17 pre-print level. This review does not change our house HOLD, $210 PT (Jul 15 initiation) — it reinforces the capex/FCF risk we flagged as the key bear-case trigger.

Read-through: Search & other accelerated to +17% YoY (vs. Q1's pace) with no visible sign yet of AI Mode/AI Overviews cannibalizing monetization — a clean answer to the disintermediation risk flagged in our preview. 3Why the Headline EPS "Beat" Is Misleading

Alphabet's $9.11 GAAP EPS print looks like a spectacular beat vs. the ~$2.89 Street consensus, but the company's own footnote disaggregates the driver: a $99.0B gross gain (net $99.03B) on equity securities — chiefly the appreciation of Alphabet's ~14% stake in Anthropic, whose implied valuation roughly tripled during the quarter (from ~$380B to ~$965B, per third-party reporting) — flowed through "Other income (expense), net." Alphabet discloses this added $21.9B to the tax provision, $77.1B to net income, and $6.26 to diluted EPS.

On this core basis, Alphabet missed by roughly $0.04 (~1.4%) — a small, arguably immaterial miss, but a meaningfully different narrative than the eye-catching $9.11 headline. This is a mark-to-market accounting gain on a minority equity stake, not operating cash flow; it is also highly volatile (the same line was a much smaller $2.66B gain a year ago) and should not be extrapolated. Investors and screeners relying on the unadjusted EPS figure will overstate both the beat and the quarter's underlying earnings quality.

Net read: the backlog and Cloud-margin data support the bull argument that this capex is demand-backed rather than speculative — but the market's reaction shows the Street is currently more focused on magnitude and cash-flow impact (quarterly FCF went negative) than on backlog quality. This is exactly the "beat-but-sell-off on capex" pattern our July 17 preview flagged as the key bear-case trigger (which previously occurred after Q4 FY2025's print), and it repeated this quarter despite Cloud actually outperforming even the bull scenario.

5Capital Allocation Shift

  • Free cash flow turned negative: -$5.86B for the quarter (operating cash flow $39.07B less capex $44.92B), the first negative quarterly FCF print in this AI-capex cycle. TTM FCF is still positive at $53.27B but the cushion is shrinking fast as capex accelerates faster than operating cash flow.
  • Buybacks paused entirely: $0 repurchased in Q2, down from $13.24B in Q2 FY25 — a clear signal capital is being redirected to infrastructure.
  • Fresh capital raised to fund the buildout: $49.6B net proceeds from a June 2026 issuance of common stock + new mandatory convertible preferred stock, plus $20.3B from senior unsecured notes. Cash & marketable securities rose to $242.5B (from $126.8B at YE2025); long-term debt roughly doubled to $98.2B.
  • New preferred dividend obligation: the Board declared a $12.15/share ($0.60/depositary share) quarterly dividend on the newly issued mandatory convertible preferred stock, alongside the existing $0.22/share common dividend — a new, structurally senior claim on cash flow that didn't exist a quarter ago.

6Read-Through to House Thesis

This print does not change our HOLD, $210 PT from the July 15 initiation report — if anything it sharpens the specific tension we flagged there. The valuation gap vs. Street consensus (~$432 average PT, "Strong Buy") is unresolved, and this quarter adds a concrete data point on both sides:

  • Supports the bear/HOLD case: capex guidance jumped ~$15-20B in three months with no matching upgrade to revenue guidance (Alphabet does not give formal revenue guidance); FCF is now negative quarterly and being backstopped by external capital raises (equity + debt) rather than fully self-funded — exactly the terminal-value/capex-supercycle risk our DCF sensitivity flagged.
  • Supports the bull/Street case: Cloud's 82% growth and $514B backlog are hard evidence the spend is demand-backed; Cloud segment margin nearly doubled YoY, showing the capex is beginning to convert to profit rather than sitting as pure cost.

We would want to see at least one more quarter of capex vs. backlog-growth data, and a clearer signal on whether FCF stabilizes or continues to deteriorate, before revisiting the rating.
