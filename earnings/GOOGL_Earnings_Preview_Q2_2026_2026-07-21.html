Setup

Alphabet reports Q2 2026 results tomorrow after the close, off the back of a blowout Q1 in which Google Cloud grew 63% YoY and cloud backlog nearly doubled sequentially to $462.3B. The stock jumped ~10% the session after that print despite a same-night after-hours dip on the capex raise — the market ultimately paid for cloud durability and shrugged off the spend. The setup into Q2 is similar: the print is less about whether Search and Cloud grow (both are expected to, comfortably into double/high-double digits) and more about whether cloud margin durability, backlog conversion, and AI-Mode monetization commentary justify the ~$180–190B 2026 capex bill — and what management says about 2027 spend, which was flagged on the Q1 call to rise "significantly."

A second, distinct issue this quarter: headline GAAP EPS is likely to be distorted by a large non-operating mark-to-market gain tied to Alphabet's Anthropic equity stake (see callout below). Investors should look through the headline beat/miss on EPS to core operating income and segment trends.

Flag — GAAP EPS likely to look distorted

BofA Securities' pre-print note estimates an ~$80B non-operating boost to Q2 operating income from the mark-to-market gain on Alphabet's Anthropic stake, pushing its own EPS estimate to ~$8.38 — far above the $2.89 Street consensus, which likely excludes or under-weights this. This mirrors Q1 2026, when GAAP EPS of $5.11 (net income +81% YoY) was inflated by similar non-operating investment gains. Do not read a large headline EPS "beat" as an operating story — reconcile to core operating income, segment margins, and TAC before drawing conclusions. This needs confirming against the 10-Q once filed.

Key metrics to watch

ranked by expected impact on the print's reaction
1
Cloud growth durability + margin trend
Consensus looks for ~$22.8B (+67%). The real question is whether the 32.9% operating margin from Q1 (up from 17.8% a year ago) holds or was helped by one-time efficiency/mix — a pullback here would undercut the "AI capex is paying off" narrative the stock has been re-rating on.
2
Backlog (RPO) growth and conversion pace
Backlog nearly doubled QoQ to $462.3B in Q1. Sell-side wants confirmation this keeps growing (not just consolidating) and color on capacity constraints — a deceleration here reads as a demand or supply signal, not just quarter-to-quarter noise.
3
AI Mode / Gemini 3 monetization commentary
Google has not disclosed AI Mode revenue, revenue-per-query, or CTR uplift. With AI Mode past 1B users and Gemini 3 having cut core AI response cost >30%, this is described by multiple desks as "the single biggest swing factor" for the stock over the next several quarters — any incremental disclosure here matters more than the ad revenue beat/miss itself.
4
FY26 capex guidance and early 2027 framing
Guide currently $180–190B. Management flagged 2027 capex to rise "significantly" on the Q1 call — the after-hours dip following that comment (before the next-day 10% rally) shows the market will punish an un-anchored capex number even inside a beat quarter.
5
YouTube ads reacceleration
Came in at $9.88B in Q1, +10.7% YoY but below Street. Consensus for Q2 is $10.76B (+9.8%) — watch for continued deceleration vs. Search, and any read-through on short-form video competitive pressure.
6
Free cash flow trajectory
Some sell-side coverage has flagged FCF compression alongside the capex ramp even as cloud revenue scales. Reconcile FCF against the 10-Q; this is a bigger swing factor for the multiple than the income-statement beat given how GAAP EPS is being distorted this quarter (see flag above).

Catalyst checklist

what actually moves the stock on the call
  1. Cloud margin print vs. 32.9%. A hold or expansion validates the AI-capex-pays-off thesis; a retreat reopens the "is this durable" debate.
  2. Backlog trajectory past $462.3B. Continued near-doubling would be extraordinary and hard to sustain — even a large absolute increase with a decelerating growth rate should be read as healthy, not a miss.
  3. Any AI Mode / agentic-search monetization disclosure. The market has been trading Alphabet largely on faith here; a concrete data point (ad load, RPM, or even qualitative framing) is the highest-value line on the call.
  4. Capex language for 2027. "Significant increase" was flagged last quarter — the market wants a number or a range, not just the adjective.
  5. Ad-tech remedies / EU litigation commentary. DOJ remedies phase (reportedly seeking AdX divestiture) and the EU appeal loss on the Android/AdTech fine are overhangs management may address unprompted.

Regulatory overhang (not this-quarter catalysts, but live)

Ad-tech antitrust (US): Following Judge Brinkema's April 2025 liability ruling that Google monopolized open-web ad tech markets, the remedies phase is active in 2026 with the DOJ reportedly seeking divestiture of Google's AdX exchange — a structural-separation outcome would be a multi-year overhang on the Network/ad-tech business, though it's a small and shrinking piece of total ads revenue.

Search antitrust choice-screen risk: Morgan Stanley's Feb 2026 note estimated mandatory choice screens could cost Google 5–8% of search traffic over three years, translating to an estimated $15–25B of annual ad revenue at risk — a slow-burn, not a Q2 catalyst, but worth a line in the note.

EU: Google lost its appeal (reported Jul 2, 2026) against a €4.1B/$4.7B EU antitrust fine — cash-immaterial for a company this size, but a reminder the multi-jurisdictional overhang isn't resolved.
