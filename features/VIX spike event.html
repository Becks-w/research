一、先定规则：什么算一次「事件」，什么算「回归均值」

VIX 是标普 500 未来 30 天的隐含波动率，长期中位数 17.6、均值 19.4。单日 +20% 是一个相当高的门槛：36 年里只有 1.4% 的交易日达到。但要注意两个口径陷阱。第一，低基数效应：VIX 在 10 附近时，涨 2 个点就是 +20%，而在 55 附近要涨 11 个点才算，所以 2017 年几次「朝鲜」跳升和 2008 年 10 月的跳升不是一个量级，报告里同时给出跳升前水平和见顶水平。第二，连续跳升要合并：2020 年 3 月三周内 6 次 +20%，2010 年 5 月一个月内 5 次，它们是同一次事件的发酵，本文把 10 个交易日内的连续跳升合并为一次事件，得到 86 次。

「回归均值」在本文用三把尺子衡量，都以首次跳升日为第 0 天、按交易日计数：①回到事件前一天的收盘水平（最严格，意味着恐慌完全消化）；②从峰值回吐一半涨幅（半衰期，衡量恐慌退潮速度）；③回到跳升前的 250 日均线（衡量相对于当年「常态」的回归）。三者的中位数分别为 12.5 天、6.5 天、9 天（中位数的取法见下框），但分布极度右偏：19% 的事件 60 天内都没回到事件前水平，因为它们本身就是波动率「换挡」的起点（2007-02、2008-09、2020-02、2018-10）。

二、事件型因素分类：谁在引爆 VIX

把 86 次事件按催化剂归为 11 类。排第一的不是战争也不是金融危机，而是「宏观数据 / 美联储」：意外加息、超预期的通胀或就业、鹰派讲话、收益率曲线倒挂，合计 24 次，占 28%。这类事件的特点是跳升当天就是顶（中位 0.5 天）、半衰期 3 天，是典型的「一次性重定价」。相反，金融危机 / 信用事件（雷曼、LTCM、法巴）跳升后中位还要再涨 9 天才见顶，回到 20 日均线要 45 天。

三、发酵路径的三种形态

按最大单日涨幅把事件分成三档，把每次事件的 VIX 除以事件前收盘价、对齐到第 0 天后取中位数，可以看到三条明显不同的路径。20–30% 档（45 次完整样本）是「脉冲」：第 0 天 +23%，第 5 天只剩 +7%，第 10 天基本归零。30–45% 档（24 次）是「余震」：跳升后仍有一两次反复，中位 17.5 天回到起点。超过 45% 的 15 次是「换挡」：中位路径在 60 天后仍高出起点 30% 以上，因为其中混着 2008、2020 这类真正的危机，也混着 2024-08、2024-12、2025-04 这类一周内回吐的假警报，两者在第 0 天看上去几乎一样。

脉冲型（单日冲击）

典型：2024-12-18 鹰派降息（+74%，4 天回到起点）、2017-05-17 科米备忘录（+46%，5 天）、2021-01-27 GameStop（+62%，5 天）。共同点是催化剂信息量在当天已经释放完毕，没有第二只靴子。

占比约 55% · 当日即顶 · 半衰期 ≤ 3 天

余震型（多日发酵）

典型：2025-04 关税（4/3 +40% → 4/4 +51% → 4/8 见顶 52 → 4/9 暂停关税单日 -36%）、2015-08 人民币贬值（三天三次跳升，第 2 天见顶）、2019-08 人民币破 7（8/5 跳升后 8/14 倒挂再来一次）。催化剂有政策博弈或对手方反应，需要 2–10 天出清。

占比约 30% · 2–10 天见顶 · 回到起点 15–40 天

换挡型（危机开端）

典型：2007-02-27（之后 7 年没回到 11）、2008-09-15（10 周后见顶 80.9）、2020-02-24（3 周 6 次跳升，见顶 82.7）、2018-10-10（11 周后 12/24 才见顶）。第一次跳升只是序章，真正的高点在 2–10 周之后。

占比约 15% · 2–10 周见顶 · 回到起点 > 100 天

七、当前热点与争议

热点 1 · 正在进行

2026-11-05：VIX +69.7% 至 28.18，中期选举后的 AI 抛售

吵什么

民主党横扫国会和德州、佐治亚州后，市场担心数据中心电价与审批监管收紧，叠加高通、Arm 财报不佳，费城半导体指数单日 -12%（2020 年 3 月以来最差），纳指 -5.5% 进入调整区。VIX 收盘从 16.61 跳到 28.18（+69.66%），是 2025 年 4 月关税冲击以来最大单日涨幅；11/6 市场反弹，VIX 回落至 25.64（-9.0%），仍比 11/4 高 54%。争议在于「够不够高」：CNBC 报道称交易员认为按纳指跌幅 VIX 本应更高；另一派认为 28 仍属有序抛售、经销商仓位均衡。

起点该算 10/16 还是 11/5

按本页的机械规则，两者是两次事件：10/16（+21.5% 至 19.99）之后 VIX 在 11/4 已跌回 16.61，比 10/16 的起点 16.46 只高 0.15，而且两次跳升间隔 14 个交易日，超过 10 天的合并阈值。但从主题看，10 月中旬的 AI 债务融资担忧、CDS 走阔、10/22 再 +14%、11/2 升到 21 的那一轮，和 11/5 的抛售是同一条 AI 估值主线，11/4 的回落只是选举结果带来的一天反弹。因此 11/5 的深度展开图改为从 10/9 起画（含 10/16），两种读法都能看：若按「10/16 起算」，这是一次已持续 15 个交易日、出现第二次 +20% 的余震/换挡型事件，参照 2018-10（10/4 +22% → 10/10 +44%）与 2025-10（10/10 +32% → 10/16 +23%）；若按「11/5 单独起算」，则参照 2024-08、2024-12 的脉冲路径。历史上「首次跳升后几乎完全回落、随后又出现更大一次跳升」的最接近案例是 2010-04（4/27 +31% → 两天后回落到 18.4 → 5/6 闪电崩盘 +32%，之后 158 天才回到起点）和 2024-07（7/24 +23% → 7/26 回到 16.4 → 8/2、8/5 两次跳升，18 天回到起点），一次换挡、一次脉冲，并不能单凭形态判定。

+69.7% 是哪个数据，为什么 VIXY 只涨 39%

+69.7% 是 CBOE 现货 VIX 指数的收盘价对收盘价涨幅（16.61 → 28.18），这个指数本身不能交易。VIXY、VXX 跟踪的是「标普 500 VIX 短期期货指数」，即第一、二个月 VIX 期货的滚动组合，而不是现货。11/4 收盘时期限结构处于贴水：现货 16.61，3 个月 VIX 18.80，6 个月 20.68，前两月期货大约在 18–20；11/5 现货冲到 28.18 时 3 个月 VIX 只到 25.55，前两月期货大约 25–27。期货从 ~19 涨到 ~26 就是 35–40%，这正是 VIXY 的 39%。换句话说，VIX 期货对现货跳升的弹性通常只有 0.5–0.6，升得越急、贴水越深，差距越大；2024-08-05 现货 +65% 当天 VIXY 也只有 +30% 左右。整周看（11/2–11/6，ETF Action 数据）：VIXY +14.2%、VXX +14.3%、UVXY（1.5 倍）+19.4%、UVIX（2 倍）+22.6%，做空的 SVIX -20.8%。

想对冲这种跳升，用什么标的更接近

没有任何 ETF 能复制现货 VIX 的单日涨幅，只能在「弹性」和「持有成本」之间选。按单日弹性从高到低：VIX 看涨期权（行权价略高于现货、到期 2–4 周）在跳升日可涨数倍，是最接近现货涨幅的工具，代价是时间价值损耗，买入后 VIX 不动就归零；UVIX（2 倍短期期货）单日约为 VIXY 的 2 倍，11/5 大约 +75%，与现货涨幅最接近，但贴水下的日滚动损耗每年可达 70–90%，只适合持有几天；UVXY（1.5 倍）介于两者之间；VIXY / VXX（1 倍）单日约为现货的一半，损耗相对低，适合作为 1–2 周的基础仓位；标普 500 看跌期权则是直接对冲股票仓位，不经过期货基差，但对波动率跳升的敏感度取决于 Vega。本页数据给出的另一个约束条件是时间：全样本 73% 的事件次日回落、脉冲型半衰期 3 天，所以对冲仓位应在跳升当天或次日了结，而不是等「回归均值」。以上是工具机制的说明，不构成投资建议。

八、对回测策略的几条可量化启示

第一，跳升当天不是做空波动率的好时点，次日才是：73% 的事件次日回落，但 34% 的事件当天就是顶也意味着另外 66% 之后还有更高收盘，且第 5 天中位数仍保留着一半的涨幅。第二，「第二次跳升」是最简单的换挡识别器：10 个交易日内再出现一次 +20%，回到起点的中位时间从 10 天变成 31.5 天、超过 60 天的概率从 9% 升到 39%，仓位规则应在第二次跳升当天切换。第三，跳升前的 VIX 水平决定基数：从 12 以下起跳的 12 次事件（2017、2014 居多）全部在 10 天内回到 20 以下；从 20 以上起跳的 28 次事件回到 20 以下的中位数是 48 天。第四，类别有信息量：宏观数据 / 美联储类半衰期 3 天，可以当脉冲处理；信用事件和疫情类必须按换挡处理。这些阈值可直接写进 VIX 相关策略的状态机，建议用本页底部数据口径复算后再用。

1. Rules first: what counts as an "event" and what counts as "mean reversion"

VIX is the implied volatility of the S&P 500 over the next 30 days; its long-run median is 17.6 and mean 19.4. A one-day +20% is a high bar: only 1.4% of trading days in 36 years clear it. Two measurement traps matter. First, the low-base effect: with VIX near 10, a 2-point move is +20%, while near 55 it takes 11 points, so the 2017 "North Korea" jumps and October 2008 are not the same magnitude; the report shows the pre-spike level and the peak alongside the percentage. Second, consecutive spikes must be merged: March 2020 had six +20% days in three weeks and May 2010 had five in a month, all part of one unfolding event. Spikes within 10 trading days of each other are merged into one event, giving 86 events.

"Mean reversion" is measured three ways, all counted in trading days from the first spike day (day 0): (1) return to the close of the day before the event (the strictest; the panic is fully digested); (2) giving back half the rise from the peak (half-life; how fast the panic ebbs); (3) return to the pre-spike 250-day moving average (reversion to that year's "normal"). The medians are 12.5, 6.5 and 9 days (see the box below for how medians are taken), but the distribution is heavily right-skewed: 19% of events had not returned to the pre-event level after 60 days, because they were the start of a volatility regime shift (2007-02, 2008-09, 2020-02, 2018-10).

Definitions used on this page

Day 0
The event's first trading day with a close-to-close rise >20%. All day counts are trading days from day 0.
Pre-spike
The close on day −1, i.e. VIX the day before the event.
Peak / days to peak
The highest close within 20 trading days from day 0, and the day it occurred (0 = the spike day itself was the top).
Half-life
After the peak, the first day VIX closes below "pre-spike + half the peak rise"; the time for half the panic to ebb. Example: pre-spike 20, peak 40, half-life is the first close ≤ 30.
Back to start
The first day after day 0 with a close ≤ the pre-spike level.
Back to 250-day MA
The first close ≤ the 250-day moving average as of the day before the event.
Lower next day
Share of events where the day-1 close is below the day-0 close.
Median
Every figure in the tables is "compute the metric for each event in the category (or the full sample), then take the median", never an average of averages. Medians are used because events like 2008 and 2020 push averages above 70 days (full-sample mean back-to-start is 76 days versus a median of 12.5). Rows with fewer than 6 events (market structure, no clear catalyst, tech/AI) are sensitive to single events and are indicative only. Events that have not yet returned, or run past the sample end, are excluded from that metric's median.

2. Catalyst types: what sets VIX off

The 86 events fall into 11 catalyst types. The largest is not war or financial crisis but "macro data / Fed": surprise hikes, hot inflation or jobs prints, hawkish speeches, curve inversions, 24 events or 28%. These typically peak on the spike day itself (median 0.5 days) with a 3-day half-life: a one-off repricing. Financial-crisis / credit events (Lehman, LTCM, BNP Paribas), by contrast, keep rising for a median 9 more days before peaking and take 45 days to get back to the 20-day average.

3. Three shapes of unfolding

Splitting events into three buckets by largest one-day rise, dividing each event's VIX by its pre-event close and aligning on day 0, the median paths differ clearly. The 20–30% bucket (45 complete events) is a "pulse": +23% on day 0, only +7% left by day 5, roughly flat by day 10. The 30–45% bucket (24 events) is an "aftershock": one or two further flare-ups, median 17.5 days back to the start. The 15 events above 45% are "regime shifts": the median path is still 30% above the start after 60 days, because the bucket mixes real crises (2008, 2020) with false alarms that unwound within a week (2024-08, 2024-12, 2025-04), and the two look almost identical on day 0.

Pulse (one-day shock)

Examples: 2024-12-18 hawkish cut (+74%, back to start in 4 days), 2017-05-17 Comey memo (+46%, 5 days), 2021-01-27 GameStop (+62%, 5 days). The catalyst's information is fully released on the day; there is no second shoe.

~55% of events · peak on day 0 · half-life ≤ 3 days

Aftershock (multi-day)

Examples: 2025-04 tariffs (4/3 +40% → 4/4 +51% → peak 52 on 4/8 → 4/9 pause, −36% in a day), 2015-08 yuan devaluation (three spikes in three days, peak on day 2), 2019-08 yuan through 7 (8/5 spike, curve inversion repeats on 8/14). Policy back-and-forth or counterparty reactions take 2–10 days to clear.

~30% · peak in 2–10 days · back to start in 15–40 days

Regime shift (start of a crisis)

Examples: 2007-02-27 (never back to 11 for seven years), 2008-09-15 (peak 80.9 ten weeks later), 2020-02-24 (six spikes in three weeks, peak 82.7), 2018-10-10 (peak only on 12/24, eleven weeks later). The first spike is a prologue; the real high comes 2–10 weeks later.

~15% · peak in 2–10 weeks · back to start > 100 days

7. Current debates

Topic 1 · ongoing

2026-11-05: VIX +69.7% to 28.18, the post-midterm AI selloff

What the argument is

After the Democratic sweep of Congress, Texas and Georgia, markets feared tighter data-center regulation and power-price politics; weak Qualcomm and Arm results added to it. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 12% in a day (worst since March 2020) and the Nasdaq fell 5.5% into correction. VIX jumped from 16.61 to 28.18 (+69.66%), the largest one-day rise since the April 2025 tariff shock; on 11/6 stocks rebounded and VIX fell to 25.64 (−9.0%), still 54% above 11/4. The debate is whether that is high enough: CNBC reported traders arguing VIX should be higher given the Nasdaq drop; others called 28 an orderly selloff with balanced dealer positioning.

Should the event start on 10/16 or 11/5?

By this page's mechanical rule they are two events: after 10/16 (+21.5% to 19.99) VIX had fallen back to 16.61 by 11/4, only 0.15 above the 10/16 starting level of 16.46, and the two spikes are 14 trading days apart, beyond the 10-day merge threshold. Thematically, though, the mid-October AI debt-financing worries, widening CDS, the +14% on 10/22 and the rise to 21 on 11/2 are the same AI-valuation thread as the 11/5 selloff; the 11/4 drop was a one-day election rebound. The 11/5 deep-dive chart therefore starts on 10/9 so both readings are visible: counted from 10/16, this is an aftershock/regime-shift event already 15 trading days old with a second +20% day, comparable to 2018-10 (10/4 +22% → 10/10 +44%) and 2025-10 (10/10 +32% → 10/16 +23%); counted from 11/5 alone, the comparables are the 2024-08 and 2024-12 pulses. The closest historical cases of "an almost complete retrace followed by a larger second spike" are 2010-04 (4/27 +31% → back to 18.4 two days later → Flash Crash +32% on 5/6, then 158 days to return) and 2024-07 (7/24 +23% → back to 16.4 on 7/26 → spikes on 8/2 and 8/5, 18 days to return): one regime shift, one pulse, so the shape alone does not settle it.

Which number is +69.7%, and why did VIXY rise only 39%

+69.7% is the close-to-close change in the CBOE spot VIX index (16.61 → 28.18), which cannot itself be traded. VIXY and VXX track the S&P 500 VIX Short-Term Futures Index, a rolling position in the first- and second-month VIX futures, not spot. At the 11/4 close the term structure was in contango: spot 16.61, 3-month VIX 18.80, 6-month 20.68, with the front two futures roughly at 18–20; on 11/5 spot reached 28.18 but the 3-month VIX only 25.55, with the front futures roughly at 25–27. Futures from ~19 to ~26 is 35–40%, which is VIXY's 39%. In other words, VIX futures typically carry only 0.5–0.6 of a spot spike, and the sharper the spike and the steeper the contango, the larger the gap; on 2024-08-05, with spot +65%, VIXY rose about 30%. For the week of 11/2–11/6 (ETF Action data): VIXY +14.2%, VXX +14.3%, UVXY (1.5x) +19.4%, UVIX (2x) +22.6%, and the short fund SVIX −20.8%.

Which instrument comes closest for hedging a spike like this

No ETF replicates spot VIX's one-day move; the choice is between sensitivity and carrying cost. In descending one-day sensitivity: VIX call options (strike slightly above the corresponding future, 2–4 weeks to expiry) can multiply several times on a spike day and come closest to spot, at the cost of time decay (they expire worthless if VIX does not move); UVIX (2x short-term futures) moves about twice VIXY, roughly +75% on 11/5, closest to the spot move, but daily roll decay in contango can reach 70–90% a year, so it is a hold of days; UVXY (1.5x) sits in between; VIXY / VXX (1x) move about half of spot with lower decay, suited to a 1–2 week base position; S&P 500 puts hedge the equity position directly without the futures basis, with sensitivity to a vol spike depending on vega. This page adds a timing constraint: 73% of events fall the next day and pulse-type half-lives are 3 days, so a hedge should be closed on the spike day or the next, not held for "mean reversion". This describes instrument mechanics and is not investment advice.

8. Quantifiable takeaways for backtesting

First, the spike day is not the time to short volatility; the next day is: 73% of events fall on day 1, but 34% peaking on day 0 means the other 66% print a higher close later, and the day-5 median still retains half the rise. Second, a "second spike" is the simplest regime-shift detector: another +20% within 10 trading days moves the median return time from 10 to 31.5 days and the probability of exceeding 60 days from 9% to 39%, so position rules should switch on the day of the second spike. Third, the pre-spike level sets the base: all 12 events starting below 12 (mostly 2017 and 2014) were back below 20 within 10 days; the 28 events starting above 20 took a median 48 days to get below 20. Fourth, category carries information: macro / Fed events have a 3-day half-life and can be treated as pulses; credit and pandemic events must be treated as regime shifts. These thresholds can go straight into a VIX strategy's state machine; recompute them from the data definitions at the bottom of this page before use.
